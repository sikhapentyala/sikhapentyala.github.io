The Lab

We build AI systems that can be trusted with sensitive data, and we test whether the guarantees they promise actually hold. The work spans privacy-preserving synthetic data generation, auditing and attacking AI systems, and applying both in healthcare and genomics.

The lab starts at the University of Washington Tacoma in September 2026. I welcome students at UW Tacoma who are curious about privacy, synthetic data, or trustworthy AI. No prior research experience needed. Have a look at the projects below and email me about the one that caught your attention. Several of them can be scoped to a single quarter as a capstone or independent study.

Current Projects

Ongoing

Privacy-preserving synthetic genomics on the NAIRR

Differentially private synthetic genomic data for open biomedical research, running on national AI research infrastructure. Uses secure multiparty computation so institutions never expose individual genetic records.

Synthetic Data PETs Genomics NAIRR

Good fit if you like cryptography, systems, or large-scale compute.

Ongoing

Synthetic genomic data for rare diseases

Generating synthetic germline data for rare conditions such as Neurofibromatosis Type 1, addressing the core problem in rare disease research: there is simply not enough data.

Synthetic Data Generative Models Genomics Healthcare

Good fit if you like generative models or computational biology.

Ongoing

Federated synthetic data generation

Generating synthetic data from records split across institutions that can never be pooled. The methods rest on cryptography: secure multiparty computation and fully homomorphic encryption, so no site ever reveals its own records to the others.

Synthetic Data Secure MPC FHE Healthcare

Good fit if you like applied cryptography or distributed systems.

Starting

Synthetic data multiplicity

What happens when you generate many synthetic datasets from the same source? Studying how utility, privacy, and bias vary run to run, and what that means for trusting any single release.

Synthetic Data Robustness Multiplicity

Good fit if you like statistics or careful experimental design.
